# Book Blurbs & Descriptions for DIADEM: BOOK I

## Amazon Kindle Description (Long Form)

**When integrity costs you everything, how much of yourself would you trade to save others?**

Cassian should have kept his head down. As a ledger-keeper in the town of Millhaven, he had one job: record the numbers, don't ask questions. But when he discovers the magistrate has been systematically overcharging the poorest district, he can't look away. He corrects the records—and loses everything.

On the day of his father's funeral, Cassian inherits more than grief and debt. Hidden in his father's belongings is an ancient medallion—warm, impossibly heavy, inscribed with alien symbols that shift before his eyes. The moment he touches it, the world transforms. He can see the invisible worth of all things: not just gold and silver, but sacrifice, suffering, hope, and truth. Every object, every person, shimmers with layers of value waiting to be understood.

But perception is only the beginning. The medallion grants him the power to broker impossible trades: exchanging anger for understanding, silence for secrets, even memories for knowledge. As Cassian learns to wield this gift, he discovers he's inherited more than a family heirloom. He's claimed a fragment of divine power—one of seven coins torn from the crown of a fallen angel.

And he's not the only one who possesses such power.

The Hollow are coming. Led by Draven Kane, a charismatic commander who wields two coins to Cassian's one, they offer desperate people relief from suffering through "fair exchange." Trade away your pain. Your grief. Your identity itself. The bargains seem generous—until victims discover they've become empty vessels, their stolen humanity feeding Draven's vast network of power.

When refugees arrive in Millhaven fleeing the Hollow's advance, Cassian faces an impossible choice: use his newfound abilities to save a few individuals, or learn to build something that can resist an army. As he struggles to free victims from Draven's network, he discovers that every rescue comes with hidden costs. Some people are too broken to save. Some victories conceal defeats. And sometimes, even when you do everything right, people still die.

Draven has spent decades perfecting the art of making damnation look like salvation. Cassian has weeks to learn what his enemy spent a lifetime mastering. To succeed, he must move beyond dramatic rescues and build a resistance that can survive without him—training others to wield the principles of his power, distributing authority instead of hoarding it, choosing patient cultivation over instant victory.

Because the greatest threat isn't Draven's army. It's the temptation to become just like him: someone who exploits desperation instead of healing it.

Perfect for fans of **Brandon Sanderson's intricate magic systems**, **Patrick Rothfuss's morally complex protagonists**, and **Mark Lawrence's strategic fantasy**, *Diadem: Book I* launches an epic series about the true cost of power and the harder path of genuine redemption. In a world where everything has a price, Cassian must discover what things are truly worth—and what he's willing to pay to save them.

## Genre-Specific Hooks

### For Fans of Complex Magic Systems:
Imagine a world where value itself is currency—where suffering, memories, and identity can be traded like gold. *Diadem* presents a magic system grounded in the Law of Balance, where every exchange must be equal, but who pays the cost is negotiable. Cassian wields fragments of divine power torn from an angel's crown, learning to perceive worth beyond price and broker impossible trades. But his enemy has weaponized the same system, creating an empire where people voluntarily surrender their humanity. The magic is logical, consistent, and morally complex—power that amplifies both heroism and exploitation.

### For Fans of Moral Complexity:
Cassian begins as a man of rigid principle: fix the ledgers, follow the law, do what's right regardless of cost. But his inherited power forces him to navigate impossible choices. Save one person and risk another's death? Free victims who may be too broken to rebuild? Use methods that mirror his enemy's exploitation? *Diadem* explores the spaces between right and wrong, where good intentions create terrible consequences, and the hardest question isn't "what should I do?" but "what am I becoming?"

### For Fans of Strategic Fantasy:
This isn't a story of chosen ones and destiny. Cassian wins through intelligence, planning, and painful adaptation. He learns that dramatic rescues don't scale. That training successors matters more than personal heroics. That building systems that survive your death is harder but more essential than winning battles yourself. Draven has spent decades building his empire of exploitation; Cassian has weeks to learn strategic thinking. The conflict is chess, not sword-fighting—every move revealing new complications, every victory concealing future costs.

## Author's Note (For Promotional Materials)

*Diadem* began with a simple question: What if value itself became visible? What if you could see what things and people are truly worth—not in gold, but in sacrifice, meaning, and human cost?

As a ledger-keeper, Cassian lives in a world of numbers and balances. But when he inherits a divine artifact that lets him perceive the invisible threads of worth in all things, he discovers that some exchanges can't be measured in coins. Pain, memories, identity itself—all have value. All can be traded. And in the hands of the wrong person, that power becomes the foundation of empire.

I wanted to explore what happens when someone fundamentally decent gains morally neutral power. How do you use a tool that can heal or exploit with equal ease? How do you resist an enemy who offers people exactly what they think they want? And how do you build something lasting in a world where dramatic gestures feel more satisfying than patient cultivation?

*Diadem* is a story about costs and consequences, about choosing the harder right over the easier wrong, and about learning that true power isn't what you can take—it's what you're willing to give away.

## Awards Submission Summary (350 words max)

*Diadem: Book I* presents an innovative approach to epic fantasy by grounding its magic system in economic and philosophical principles of value and exchange. The novel follows Cassian, a ledger-keeper whose act of professional integrity costs him everything—then grants him something far more dangerous: a divine artifact that allows him to perceive and negotiate the invisible worth of all things.

The magic system operates on the Law of Balance: all exchanges must be equal, but what constitutes "value" extends far beyond material wealth. Pain, memories, identity itself—all carry quantifiable worth and can be traded. This creates a morally complex framework where the same power can heal or exploit depending on the wielder's intent. Cassian must master this system while resisting Draven Kane, a antagonist who has weaponized it to build an empire where desperate people voluntarily trade their humanity for relief.

What distinguishes *Diadem* is its commitment to exploring power's true costs. Cassian doesn't win through destiny or superior strength, but through strategic thinking, painful adaptation, and the recognition that sustainable change requires distributing power rather than centralizing it. The novel interrogates common fantasy tropes: chosen ones, dramatic rescues, instant transformations—and offers instead a protagonist who learns that building systems that survive your death matters more than personal heroics.

The worldbuilding is both epic in scope and intimate in detail, featuring characters whose moral complexity drives the narrative forward. Draven isn't evil for evil's sake; he genuinely believes he's offering salvation. Victims of his network aren't simply waiting to be saved; some are too broken to fully restore, forcing Cassian to confront the limits of his power and the weight of his choices.

Thematically, *Diadem* explores how good people navigate morally ambiguous power, the tension between urgent needs and sustainable solutions, and the question of what we're willing to sacrifice—not to gain power, but to give it away responsibly.

The novel stands as the first in a planned seven-book series, each exploring different aspects of divine power and the humans who must choose whether to wield them as tools of liberation or instruments of control.
