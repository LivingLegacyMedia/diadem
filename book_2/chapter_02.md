# Chapter 2: Learning to See

The grass died first.

Cassian sat cross-legged in the garden behind the safe house, both coins active, watching a few blades around him turn from green to brown to gray. His first attempt—crude, forceful. He'd pulled too hard, demanded instead of asked, and the plants had given everything until there was nothing left.

But that wasn't the discovery.

He tried again. This time, gentler. Not taking, but *connecting*. The Coin of Seeds formed a link with the healthy lawn around him—dozens of grass plants, vibrant and alive. Through the Coin of Heads, he opened gray space and directed that mental strain outward through the connection.

The grass responded.

Not by dying. By *sharing*. Their vitality—the energy they would have used for growth, for repair, for simple existence—flowed into the network. And Cassian used it to fuel his expanded consciousness. The gray space stabilized. Deepened. Instead of forcing his own brain to bear the impossible strain of slowed time, he was distributing that computational load across hundreds of living plants.

A brain trust.

The world froze around him. A bird hung in the air mid-flight. Dust motes stopped drifting. And Cassian sat at the center of it all, his mind operating at impossible speeds while the grass around him processed fragments of the burden.

Five minutes. Ten. Fifteen.

When he finally released the gray space, his head didn't pound. His vision didn't blur. He felt... fine. Tired, yes. But not the bone-deep exhaustion that usually followed coin use.

And the grass was still green. Slightly paler, perhaps. The plants had given energy they would have spent on growth. But they were alive. Healthy. Recovering already as sunlight fed them more vitality.

This was sustainable.

Three days of experimentation had led to this. Three days alone, testing theories, pushing boundaries, discovering what happened when you used both coins together instead of separately.

The door to the garden opened. Torin stepped out, studying the small circle of withered grass near Cassian's feet, then the vibrant green lawn surrounding it.

"You've been busy," he said.

Cassian stood, brushing dirt from his pants. "I found something. Several things, actually." He gestured at the contrast. "The dead grass there? That's what happens when I take too much. But the rest..." He indicated the living lawn. "That's what happens when I connect properly. Using plant life as a network makes everything more efficient. The Coin of Seeds creates the link, the Coin of Heads distributes mental strain across it."

"So you're trading with plants now?"

"Sort of." Cassian activated the Coin of Seeds again, focusing on a nearby rosebush. "Healthy plants can lend me their vitality for mental tasks—thinking, perception, gray space. The energy they'd use for growth gets temporarily redirected to my consciousness. As long as I'm only using it for mental work, they barely notice. It's sustainable." He paused. "But that's just the first discovery. Watch this."

He didn't broker a trade this time. Just tapped into the coin's passive ability—direct manipulation of plant matter. The rosebush shuddered. Branches extended. Buds formed, bloomed, scattered petals in seconds. The growth was rapid, unnatural, beautiful.

"I've done that before," Cassian said. "Making plants grow. It's one of the coin's basic abilities. But then I tried something else."

He activated the Coin of Heads while keeping the Coin of Seeds engaged with the rosebush.

Gray space opened. Time slowed. And suddenly he could *feel* the rosebush. Not just manipulate it, but perceive through it. The roots stretching underground. The cells dividing. The water moving through xylem and phloem. And deeper—the rosebush's own form of awareness. Not consciousness like humans had, but something. Responsiveness. Processing.

"It's like having a distributed nervous system," Cassian explained, maintaining both coins. "The gray space expands my perception. The Seeds coin connects me to plants. Together..." He focused, extending his awareness. "I can sense through living plant matter. Every blade of grass, every weed, every tree—they become extensions of my consciousness. They process information for me."

Torin was quiet for a moment. "That's... useful."

"More than useful." Cassian released both coins. "I've been testing it. The range extends as far as there are plants. And it's not just sensing their biology—I can perceive *through* them. What they touch, what's near them. They detect light, vibration, pressure. It's like having sensors everywhere something grows."

"Reconnaissance," Torin said.

"Yes, but there's more." Cassian sat on the garden bench. "Yesterday I tried something different. Used the plant network while perceiving a person nearby. One of the volunteers who's still here. And I noticed—my ability to read his thoughts, to sense his mind through the Coin of Heads, it reached *further* when I was connected to plants. Almost like the plants acted as..."

"A network," Torin finished.

"Exactly." Cassian leaned forward. "The Coin of Heads governs the mind—thoughts, memories, perception. But its range is limited. I can read someone's thoughts if they're close enough. Communicate telepathically with people bound to my network. But only within maybe fifty yards normally."

"And with plants?"

"I tested it this morning. Connected to the garden, then tried to sense the volunteer inside the house. Usually I'd need to be in the same room. But through the plant network—with their vitality supporting my mental reach—I could sense his mind from out here. The plants extended my range and shouldered the mental load."

Torin studied him. "You've discovered synergies."

"Exactly. Using my coins separately, I understand their individual power. But together..." Cassian looked at his hands. "Together they create capabilities neither one has by itself. I can distribute mental strain across a living network. Use their vitality to fuel consciousness instead of exhausting myself. They shoulder the burden of expanded perception. As long as I only ask them to help with *thinking*, they can sustain it almost indefinitely."

"And if you ask for more?" Torin's eyes went to the dead grass.

"Then they die." Cassian's voice was quiet. "If I use their vitality for physical things—healing myself, accelerating my own body's processes—it drains them too quickly. Takes more than they can spare. That's what happened at first. I was pulling energy to heal exhaustion, repair strain. The plants couldn't sustain it."

"But they can help you...think."

"In a way, thinking is what plants already do. They process light. Respond to stimuli. Distribute resources through their structure. It's not so different from neural processing." Cassian stood. "When I ask them to help me *think*, it's asking them to do what they already do, just... directed. Focused. It's sustainable."

Torin was quiet for a moment. "You're wondering what else you're missing."

"I'm wondering what the makers of the coins knew that we've forgotten." Cassian activated the Coin of Seeds, focusing on the dead grass. He reversed the flow—took his own vitality, his own energy, and pushed it into the withered plants. They shuddered. Color returned. Green pushing through gray. "I can give back what I take. Revitalize what I've drained. But it costs me." The grass was alive again, recovering. Cassian felt the drain on his own reserves. "I can sustain mental enhancement almost indefinitely with healthy plants. But healing or restoration? That requires more sacrifice. A strain on my energy and health."

Torin studied him for a long moment. "The discoveries are impressive. The synergies are real." He glanced at the garden, at the circle of recovering grass, at the manipulated rosebush. "But this is still just the beginning. You've learned how to use the tools. You still need to figure out *what to build* with them."

Cassian nodded slowly. "You're right. I've been thinking too small. Individual rescues, tactical strikes—even with better methods, it's not enough."

"So what's the next step?"

"I need time to think. Real time. The garden gives me minutes of enhanced thought, but..." Cassian looked at the living plants around him. "I need more. Need to work through the larger problem properly. Figure out how to actually *win* this, not just survive it."

Torin's expression shifted slightly. Not quite approval, but close. "For what it's worth—you're thinking now instead of just charging ahead. Keep doing that."

Then he was gone.

Cassian stood alone in the garden. Around him, the dead grass was already showing hints of green, new growth pushing through the gray husks. Life returning.

He'd discovered how his coins could work together. How to sustain himself without burning out. How to distribute mental strain across living networks.

But Torin was right. He was still thinking tactically when he needed to think strategically.

Time to change that.

---

Cassian sat in the garden for a long time after Torin left, turning the problem over in his mind.

He could free individuals. One at a time, slowly, with plant support making the process sustainable. But that wasn't a strategy—it was triage. Draven had thousands of Hollow. Even if Cassian freed ten a day without exhausting himself, it would take months. Years. And Draven would be making new ones faster than Cassian could free the old.

The plant network gave him surveillance. Enhanced thinking. The ability to distribute cognitive load. But what good was that if he was still approaching this the same way? Charging at Emissaries, liberating small groups, winning tactical victories while losing the strategic war.

He needed an advantage. Something to tip the scales.

Cassian's hand went to his belt. To the wrapped bundle he'd been carrying since Arvenstead. The Coin of Waters. Draven's coin, taken during their battle. Still bound to the tyrant, wrapped in oilcloth that helped isolate it from Draven's influence.

He'd been avoiding it. Afraid that even touching it would alert Draven to his location. Afraid of the connection it represented.

But maybe that was the wrong approach.

Cassian pulled the bundle from his belt. Unwrapped it carefully. The coin gleamed in the fading light—silver-blue, etched with flowing patterns that seemed to shift like water.

Through the Coin of Heads, he could perceive the binding. A tether stretching from the coin toward Draven, miles away in the capital. Not a simple thread like the Hollow carried—this was fundamental. The coin *belonged* to Draven. Had accepted him as its bearer.

But it was here. In Cassian's hand. Physical possession, even without bonding.

What could he do with that?

The question was too big. Too complex. He needed to think it through properly. But the garden around him—lush offered limited processing power. Dozens of plants. Maybe a few hundred square feet of greenery.

Not enough.

Cassian's thoughts went to the Thornwood. The ancient forest that sprawled north of the capital, hours of travel into the wilderness. He'd passed through its edges during his flight from the reservoirs. Trees that had stood for centuries. Dense undergrowth. Vitality so thick you could practically taste it in the air.

If the garden gave him fifteen minutes of enhanced thought...

What could a forest give him?

Hours? Days?

Cassian stood, decision crystallizing. He wrapped the Coin of Waters again and secured it to his belt. Collected his pack from inside the safe house. Left a note for Torin: *Gone north. Back in a few days.*

Then he started walking.

---

The Thornwood began as a thinning of farms and a thickening of trees. Cassian reached the true forest edge by late afternoon the next day, having pushed through the night and morning with only brief rests.

The difference was immediate.

Even without activating the Coin of Seeds, he could feel the vitality. Towering oaks with trunks wider than houses. Maples and ash trees competing for canopy space. Underneath, a carpet of ferns and moss and flowering undergrowth. Everything green and alive and *old*.

Some of these trees had been growing since ancient days. Before Draven. Before the Guild fell. They'd witnessed centuries, and they were still here, patient and enduring.

Cassian walked deeper. Away from roads and trails, into the oldest parts where humans rarely ventured. The forest canopy closed overhead, filtering sunlight into green-tinted dimness.

Finally, he found it: a clearing formed by a fallen giant. The ancient tree had come down years ago, creating a gap in the canopy. Younger trees were already competing to fill the space, but for now it was open to sky. Surrounded by healthy, mature forest on all sides.

Perfect.

Cassian set down his pack. Pulled out the wrapped Coin of Waters and placed it on the moss in front of him. Then he sat cross-legged, hands on his knees, and activated both coins.

The Coin of Seeds reached out.

The forest *responded*.

Not dozens of plants. Not hundreds. *Thousands*. Every tree within two hundred yards. Every fern. Every moss-covered stone. Every hidden root system spreading through soil. The vitality was staggering—centuries of accumulated growth, interconnected webs of life that made the garden seem like a child's toy.

Cassian formed connections carefully. Asked, didn't demand. *Share with me. Help me think. Lend me your processing.*

The forest shared.

Through the Coin of Heads, Cassian opened gray space.

And his mind *exploded*.

Not painfully. Not overwhelmingly. Just... *expanded*. As if his consciousness had been compressed into a single room his entire life, and someone had suddenly removed the walls.

The world stopped. A bird frozen mid-flight overhead. Wind-rustled leaves caught in stillness. Even the play of light through canopy seemed suspended.

But Cassian's thoughts raced.

With the garden, he'd had minutes of enhanced cognition. With the forest supporting him—thousands of trees sharing vitality, distributing the computational load—he had *hours*. Maybe days, subjectively, if he could maintain the connection.

Time to think.

Time to plan.

Time to solve the unsolvable.

Cassian looked at the wrapped coin before him. Let his enhanced mind work through the problem from every angle.

*Draven has the Coin of Flames and the Coin of Blades. I have the Coin of Heads and the Coin of Seeds. Draven also has thousands of Hollow tethered to him, giving him fuel to burn. I have... plants.*

*But I also have his third coin.*

Even wrapped, he could sense it. The binding was there—Draven's bond, claiming ownership. But the coin itself was neutral. An artifact. A tool.

Cassian released gray space. Let time resume normal flow. Whatever he was going to do with the Coin of Waters, he needed to do it physically. Actually try things, not just contemplate them.

He unwrapped the coin slowly. Silver-blue, etched with flowing patterns like water. Through the Coin of Heads, the tether to Draven pulsed—active, aware.

Every time someone had tried to touch Cassian's coins, they'd felt burning. Pain. Rejection.

Only one way to know if the same applied in reverse.

Cassian reached out and picked up the Coin of Waters.

Pain exploded through his hand. Searing agony, like gripping red-hot metal. The coin SCREAMED rejection through the bond. Wrong wrong wrong, this bearer isn't yours, release it NOW.

Cassian's vision whited out. His muscles locked. The forest clearing spun.

But through the pain, something else: moisture condensing in the air. Mist forming around him. Dew appearing on leaves where none had been seconds before.

The coin was active. Doing *something*.

Cassian activated the Coin of Seeds with his free hand. Connected to the plants around him. And suddenly—

Water. He could FEEL water. In every plant. In the air. Underground streams flowing beneath roots. The moisture content of bark and leaf and moss became visible, tangible, *accessible*.

The Coin of Heads flared. Gray space opened involuntarily, triggered by the combined coin activation.

For three seconds, Cassian perceived the entire forest through its water content. Miles of interconnected hydraulic systems. Sap flowing. Roots drinking. The forest was a massive liquid network, and he could sense all of it.

Then the pain became too much.

He dropped the coin. Gasped. Fell to his knees, cradling his burned hand. The skin was red, blistered where he'd gripped the silver-blue surface.

Three seconds. That's all he'd managed.

But he'd felt it. The synergy. Three coins working together created something none of them had alone.

Cassian sat back, breathing hard. Looked at the coin lying on moss. At his blistered palm. At the mist that still hung in the air around him, slowly dissipating.

Three seconds wasn't enough. The pain made concentration impossible. Even with gray space amplifying his cognition, the burning overwhelmed everything.

He needed another approach.

---

Cassian pulled a waterskin from his pack. Emptied it into a depression in the moss, creating a small pool. Then he dropped the Coin of Waters into the water.

The coin sank. Settled at the bottom, submerged under two inches of liquid.

Cassian put his hand in the water.

Contact, but not direct. The water touched the coin. His hand touched the water. Maybe the liquid would act as a buffer, dilute the rejection somehow.

He concentrated.

The pain came anyway. Duller, yes—filtered through liquid medium—but still there. Burning worked its way up through water, through his palm, into his wrist.

But the coin activated.

The water in the moss-pool began moving. Swirling. Not from wind or disturbance, but from the coin itself. Cassian felt its passive ability reaching out—sensing liquid, manipulating it, drawing moisture from surrounding air.

He activated the Coin of Seeds.

Connection flowed through his other hand into the forest network. And through the water in the moss-pool, through the submerged coin, he felt the two abilities merge. Plants that had roots near groundwater lit up in his awareness. The water inside their cells became tangible.

Gray space opened.

Better. Clearer. The water diluted the pain enough that he could actually think. Maintain concentration. Process what he was sensing.

The forest's hydraulic network became transparent. He could trace underground water. Sense how trees communicated through dissolved chemicals in soil moisture. Feel the way rain had soaked into humus layers and was slowly being drawn up through root systems.

Ten seconds this time. Then fifteen. Twenty.

But the pain was building. Even filtered through water, the coin's rejection intensified. Cassian's vision started to blur. His hand cramped.

He pulled it out. Gasped. Twenty seconds. Better than three, but still not sustainable.

And through the Coin of Heads, he felt it: Draven's attention turning this direction. The tyrant had noticed someone touching his coin. Twice now.

Cassian retrieved the Coin of Waters from the pool. Dried it quickly. His hand throbbed—new blisters forming over old.

Better, but not good enough.

---

Third attempt.

Cassian held the Coin of Heads in his right hand. His bonded coin. The Coin of Waters in his left, pressed directly against his palm.

Maybe one coin could buffer the other. Transfer authority somehow. Or the Heads coin could help his mind process the rejection, make it tolerable.

He brought his hands together. Pressed both coins into contact with each other.

The coins *repelled*.

Not subtly. They physically pushed apart, like matching poles of magnets. Cassian had to force them together, and even then they vibrated with rejection. Wrong. Bonded to different bearers. They refused to touch.

The moment contact broke, pain lanced through both hands. Not just the Waters coin burning—now the Heads coin ALSO flared hot. Angry. Defending its own bond.

Cassian dropped both. Shook out his hands. Backed away.

That was worse. The coins actively rejected each other. Wouldn't interface that way.

---

Fourth attempt.

Cassian picked up the Coin of Waters with cloth this time. Wrapped it in fabric from his pack to avoid direct skin contact. Then he opened his mouth and placed the wrapped coin on his tongue.

Inside his head. Touching saliva—a liquid. He bore the Coin of Heads, and that coin governed his skull, his brain. Maybe the combination would create a loophole. Head-liquid-coin. A chain that gave him access.

He activated both of his bonded coins.

Nothing.

The cloth muffled everything. No passive abilities. No water sensing. The fabric was non-conductive. Blocking the coin's function entirely.

Cassian removed the cloth wrapping. Placed the bare coin directly on his tongue.

Pain. Immediate, overwhelming. The inside of his mouth burned. His tongue went numb. He couldn't breathe—throat closing, rejecting the foreign object.

And through the agony: water. He could taste it. Actually TASTE every liquid in his body. Blood. Saliva. Cerebrospinal fluid. The moisture in his tissues became hyper-present, overwhelming his senses.

The Coin of Heads flared. Gray space tried to open but couldn't—his consciousness was drowning in sensation. Too much input. Pain plus overwhelming liquid-awareness plus his tongue burning and—

Cassian spat the coin out. Coughed. Fell forward, gasping. His mouth tasted like copper and ash.

Worse. Much worse. That approach didn't create a loophole. It just made the rejection more intimate, more agonizing.

---

Fifth attempt.

Near the clearing's edge, a small stream trickled between mossy rocks. Cassian walked to it. Found where water pooled in a natural depression.

Algae grew in the pool. Green film coating rocks. Simple plant life that lived in water, drew nutrients from water, existed as part of the aquatic ecosystem.

Cassian placed the Coin of Waters at the bottom of the pool. Three inches of stream water covered it.

Then he knelt at the pool's edge. Activated the Coin of Seeds. Reached out to the algae.

Connection formed. Plant to bearer. The algae was alive, responsive. It grew in water, was surrounded by water, drew sustenance from water.

And the coin sat beneath it.

Cassian pushed the connection deeper. Used the algae as an interface. The plant touched water. Water touched the coin. The chain linked: Cassian through Seeds to plant, plant through its medium to water, water to Waters coin.

A translation chain.

Cassian bore the Coin of Seeds. The algae responded to Seeds. The algae lived in water, was part of the water. The water touched the Coin of Waters. Each step translated authority from one domain to the next.

Something shifted.

The pain came, but distant. Wrong, but not overwhelming. The algae bore the direct rejection—it was touching water that touched the coin. Cassian only felt the echo, filtered through two layers.

And the passive abilities activated.

Through the algae, through the water, Cassian felt the Coin of Waters respond. He could sense liquid throughout the stream. Control it. Make it move.

He concentrated. The water in the pool began to swirl. Not a ripple from the wind. From his will, channeled through the coin's power. Moisture condensed in the air above the stream. Mist formed, thickened, became almost solid before dissipating.

He could manipulate water. Direct its movement. Pull moisture from air.

Cassian activated the Coin of Heads.

Gray space opened—and kept opening.

Normally, gray space gave him seconds of slowed time before exhaustion. With plant support, he'd managed fifteen minutes in the garden. With the forest, hours.

Now, with the Coin of Waters also active through the algae-chain, time didn't just slow. It nearly stopped.

The stream's babbling went silent. Individual water droplets hung frozen mid-splash. Even the mist he'd created became absolutely still.

Three coins. Three domains. The mental strain distributed across plants (Seeds), the time dilation amplified by water (Waters), the perception enhanced by consciousness (Heads). They stacked. Multiplied each other.

Cassian existed in a moment that lasted subjectively forever. He could think. Plan. Observe. The forest supported his cognition. The water amplified the time dilation. His consciousness processed it all.

This was how you fought alone and won. Not by being stronger. By being impossibly fast. By having hours to react while opponents had seconds.

He held the connection for a full minute of real time. Subjectively, it felt like half a day. Then the algae began to brown.

Taking too much strain. The translation chain was killing it.

Cassian released the connection. Gray space collapsed. Time resumed. The stream bubbled. The algae was dead, blackened, floating in pieces.

But he'd learned something that might change everything.

---

Cassian sat by the stream, hands shaking. Examined what he'd discovered:

The Coin of Waters gave him water control. Mist, rain, movement of liquids.

But more importantly: it amplified gray space. Stacked with Seeds and Heads to create time dilation that was almost infinite. He'd had half a subjective day in one real minute.

That changed everything about combat. About planning. About survival.

The plant-water chain worked. Clunky, yes. Required setup—finding the right intermediary, positioning everything correctly. But it worked.

Cassian looked at his blistered hands. At the dead algae. At the wrapped coin in his pack.

He couldn't carry this into battle easily. Couldn't just pull out the coin mid-fight and activate it. The setup required water, living plants that interfaced with water, time to establish the connection.

But if he prepared? Found the right location? Set up the chain in advance?

He could have nearly infinite thinking time. Could perceive everything. Could control water and mist and rain while his mind operated at impossible speeds.

Cassian activated the Coin of Seeds. Pushed vitality into the dead algae. Watched green slowly return. Fair exchange—the plant had died translating for him. The least he could do was try to bring it back.

But one successful test wasn't enough. The algae-chain worked, but it required a stream, a pool, stationary setup. He couldn't carry that into battle. Couldn't use it on the move.

He needed something portable.

The forest around him was still connected, still sharing vitality for cognitive support. Cassian re-entered gray space—just with his own two coins now, without Waters active.

Time slowed moderately. His thoughts accelerated. He worked through possibilities.

A waterskin wouldn't work. Dead leather. No living plant to form the translation chain.

But what about a gourd? Living plant material. Could be filled with water. Could act as both container and translator.

Or a section of bamboo. Hollow, water-tight, technically still alive if kept properly.

Or even simpler: a water-filled clay jar with moss growing on the inside. The moss would be the living plant, the water the medium, the coin submerged in both.

Cassian released gray space. Looked around the clearing. Near the stream, wild gourds grew on vines. Not large, but functional. He picked one—still green, still attached to its vine—and used his knife to carefully cut it free while keeping the stem intact.

The gourd was alive. Barely, but enough.

He cut a small opening in the top. Scooped out the seeds. Then walked to the stream and filled the gourd with water, watching it slosh inside the hollow cavity.

Cassian unwrapped the Coin of Waters. Dropped it through the opening into the water-filled gourd.

Then he held the gourd in one hand. Activated the Coin of Seeds with the other, reaching for the living gourd itself.

Connection formed. Cassian to gourd. Gourd to water inside it. Water to coin.

The translation chain activated.

Slight pain, but tolerable. And the passive abilities responded. Mist began forming around the gourd's opening. Cassian could sense moisture in the air, in nearby plants, in the stream.

He activated the Coin of Heads. Gray space opened—and time nearly stopped.

The gourd worked. Mobile. Portable. He could carry this.

Cassian held the connection for thirty seconds of real time before the gourd started to brown. Dying, like the algae. But thirty seconds of near-infinite subjective time was substantial.

He released the connection. Pulled the coin from the gourd carefully. The plant was withering, but it had proven the concept.

Mobile access to all three coins. Clunky, yes—he'd need to prepare fresh gourds regularly, or find something more durable. But functional.

Cassian wrapped the Coin of Waters again. Secured it. Then used the Coin of Seeds to accelerate growth on several gourd vines nearby. More fruit forming, ripening quickly. He'd take a few with him. Test different containers. See what lasted longest.

He had work to do. Find the best portable setup. Tell Torin what he'd discovered. Figure out how to actually use these tools.

Surveillance over miles. Water manipulation. Near-infinite thinking time.

And now, a way to access it all while moving.

---

Cassian released the gray space gradually. Let the forest's vitality flow back into growth and life. The world resumed its normal speed. Wind rustled leaves. A bird called overhead.

He sat in the clearing, breathing hard despite not having moved. His mind felt stretched. Used. But not exhausted. The forest had borne most of the burden.

Around him, the trees looked slightly paler. Not dead. Not even withered. Just tired. They'd given him hours of subjective thought in exchange for energy they would have used for growth.

Cassian wrapped the Coin of Waters carefully. Secured it. Shouldered his pack.

Then he started walking back toward the safe house.

He had work to do. Preparations to make. A strategy to refine.

But for the first time since Arvenstead, he felt something other than desperation.

He felt like he might actually have a chance.
